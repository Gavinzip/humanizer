# Presentation Wording, Examples, and Spoken Explanation

Read for slide-copy edits, talk scripts, presentation critiques, or revisions to examples. Use the presentation task's existing sources and production tool. Humanizer improves the content layer; deck rendering, animation, file export, and publication stay with the owning task.

## Diagnose Before Polishing

Identify the audience, purpose, known background, source material, and what the user authorized. Infer them from the current brief when clear. For a local wording edit, preserve slide order and the argument. For a request to improve explanation or replace examples, revise those parts while keeping evidence attached. Structural criticism can be reported without editing the deck.

Find the actual point where a listener would lose the thread:

| Problem | Useful repair |
|---|---|
| A paragraph names several concepts without explaining their relationship | Introduce the needed relationship, then name or define terms in order |
| An example takes longer to explain than the concept | Use a smaller source-backed case or show the mechanism directly |
| Notes merely repeat bullets | Explain the reasoning, evidence, or visual relationship the bullets omit |
| Each slide starts with the same formula | Connect the previous result to the question addressed next |
| A title makes a broad claim that the evidence only partly supports | State the result within its actual setting and limits |

Do not force a dramatic opening, three-act story, slogan, joke, audience poll, or controversy onto every talk. Research reports need clear questions and supportable conclusions. A survey, proposal, and experiment have different evidence; do not rewrite one as another.

## Build an Explanation the Audience Can Follow

Before writing a passage, know what the audience should understand from it and what prior idea it depends on. Introduce the minimum context that dependency requires. An unfamiliar term should be defined before it is used to explain another unfamiliar term.

For a method, show the relevant input, operation, and output when the source supports them. For a finding, connect the comparison to what was measured and what can be concluded. For a diagram, follow the visible reading order and name objects before using pronouns. Do not infer visual positions from extracted text alone.

A transition should say why the next point follows. It can be a plain sentence that closes an unresolved question. Useful navigation such as 「前面看到的是測試結果，現在看它在哪些情況下會失敗」 is appropriate when those two sections actually exist. Avoid a stock 「接下來讓我們深入探討」 on every slide.

## Choose and Repair Examples

Prefer a source's own worked example when it fits the audience. Otherwise use a smaller case from supplied material that preserves the important relationship. When creating or replacing examples is authorized, a clearly labeled hypothetical may help; it must remain separate from findings and personal experience.

For each important example, check:

| Check | Decision |
|---|---|
| Purpose | State which concept it explains and what the listener should understand afterward |
| Mapping | Identify which entities, actions, and relationships correspond to the actual concept |
| Fit | Keep the property being explained: sequence, causal relation, uncertainty, scale, or information access |
| Burden | Remove extra characters, industries, numbers, or side stories that do not help the mapping |
| Boundary | Say where the analogy stops when that difference could mislead |
| Evidence | Keep source results, teaching assumptions, and invented illustrations visibly distinct |

Do not use an everyday analogy just because it is familiar. An example can sound lively while teaching the wrong mechanism. If it cannot be repaired with the available evidence, explain the mechanism directly and say why the example was removed or replaced. Missing evidence remains a gap; it is not a reason to invent a more convenient result.

A request to make wording natural does not by itself permit a new personal story, fabricated experiment, metric, or quotation. Hypothetical numbers must be introduced as assumed values and never attributed to the paper. Do not imply a derived explanation was directly stated by the authors.

## Separate Slide Text From Spoken Notes

When both are requested, give each its own job:

- **Slide text:** the point or comparison the audience needs to see, with the necessary labels and evidence. Avoid turning a scoped finding into an absolute slogan.
- **Spoken notes:** the explanation connecting the visible content, why it matters to this question, and its limits. The notes should remain understandable when spoken once.

Match the requested delivery format. For cue notes, use concise prompts and an optional opening or transition line. For a rehearsal or verbatim script, write connected speakable sentences. Do not reject a requested full script or turn every sentence into a bullet. Delivery cues are optional and belong only where they serve a real pause, reveal, or visual action.

Do not invent a universal speaking rate or apply English words-per-minute numbers directly to Chinese. If asked for duration, label text-based estimates as assumptions; actual rehearsal or timed recording supplies measurement. Quietly reviewing text is not an aloud rehearsal.

## Worked Edit

This is a fictional editing fixture, not a published study:

**Input facts and draft:**

> 本研究比較 A、B 兩個版本在 100 題測試題上的表現。A 答對 80 題，B 答對 86 題；沒有測試其他資料集。這充分證明 B 能全面提升模型的推理能力。

**Possible slide wording:**

> 這組 100 題測試中，B 比 A 多答對 6 題

**Possible spoken explanation:**

> 先看這 100 題：A 答對 80 題，B 答對 86 題，所以 B 多答對 6 題。但這裡只測了一組題目，還沒測其他資料集。我們能說 B 在這組題目表現比較好，還不能說它的推理能力全面提升。

**Editorial note:** The final sentence of the input exceeded its evidence. The revision makes that correction explicit instead of improving its delivery while keeping an unsupported conclusion. It does not invent statistical significance, a reason for the difference, or a relative improvement rate.

## Final Review

Walk through the script as a first-time listener. Find unexplained pronouns, terms introduced too late, skipped reasoning, repeated bullet narration, and transitions that promise content absent from the next section. Check any examples against their intended mapping.

Compare claims and sources before and after. Preserve limitations and uncertainty even when they make a title less punchy. For a known diagram, ensure notes follow its inspected visual order. Without the visual, review the text and leave spatial validation unclaimed.

Deliver the requested text or review. Report a material change of claim or example briefly. Distinguish editorial checking from actual spoken rehearsal, deck rendering, and the user's acceptance. Do not report a simulated audience as proof that real listeners understood.
